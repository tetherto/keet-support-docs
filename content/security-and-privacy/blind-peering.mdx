Keet has [no central servers](/#how-does-keet-work-without-central-servers) — a room lives on the devices of the people in it. That raises a fair question: **if I send a message while the other person is offline, and then I go offline too, how does it ever arrive?**

## The problem

To sync a room, someone holding a copy of it has to be online. Most phones and laptops aren't online all the time — so two people who are never online at the same moment would never catch up on each other's messages.

## The solution: blind peers

A **blind peer** is a peer on the network that is simply **more available than most devices** — it stays online so a room's data stays reachable.

When you send a message, a blind peer keeps a copy. When other members of the room come online — even long after you've gone offline — they sync the latest messages from whoever holds them, including the blind peer.

![How blind peering works: your message is stored in encrypted form by an always-on blind peer, and your friend syncs it when they come online. The blind peer cannot decrypt anything it stores.](/images/security-and-privacy/blind-peering-flow.svg "Blind Peering")

## But can't that peer read my messages?

No — that's why it's called *blind*.

A Keet room is **encrypted**, and only the people in the room have the keys. A blind peer stores the room's data in its encrypted form and has no key to unlock it: it can't read your messages, files, or names. It helps keep the room available — it can never see inside it.

<Callout type="info">
A blind peer isn't a company server. It's just another peer on the network — anyone can run one. 🌐
</Callout>
